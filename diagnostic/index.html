Diagnostic

Find your software starting point

Four questions about your systems, project stage, objective and type of work. Get a suggested next step to discuss with your team.

This is a qualitative orientation. It does not measure readiness, predict returns or certify a system for production.

Before you talk to vendors, decide what to ask

A vendor conversation tends to start with the vendor’s product. A short diagnostic helps you start with your own problem, so you can ask better questions and compare proposals on the same terms.

What changes

You describe the problem first

Bring your workflow, constraints and intended outcome into the conversation. Use these to explore fit beyond a feature list.

You ask the same questions of everyone

A shared starting point can help you compare scope, dependencies and responsibilities across proposals.

You know what you don’t know yet

Make open questions visible, from data access to ongoing ownership. Use them to decide what evidence to ask for next.

Questions the diagnostic helps you prepare

  • Which part of our workflow does this change, and who owns it afterwards?
  • What do you need from us — data, access or people — before you can start?
  • What evidence would we look for in the first month to assess whether this is working?
  • What happens to our data and systems if we stop working together?
  • Which parts are configuration, and which are custom work we will have to maintain?

These four answers offer a qualitative starting point for discussion; vendor due diligence still needs evidence, technical review and agreed terms.
