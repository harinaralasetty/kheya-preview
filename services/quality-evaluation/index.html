The idea behind this service

Evidence before release

Define acceptable behaviour, gather evidence at each layer and keep watching after release.

Evidence before release connects each important risk to a check and a named decision owner. A release gate is the review where those owners examine results and gaps, including reasons to hold a release; continued measurement belongs in the operating plan.

What it is

Quality evaluation asks whether the available evidence supports a system’s intended use. It begins with requirements and risks, then examines what existing tests establish and where further checks or evaluation examples are needed.

The result can support a release, a narrower use or a decision to hold. Passing checks establishes only the behaviour examined; this assessment does not certify legal compliance, eliminate failures or make Kheya the operator of monitoring or incident response.

What winning looks like

Coverage of the agreed risks

Does each important requirement or failure mode have a relevant check, with untested areas named rather than hidden behind a total test count?

You would see: Inspect a risk-to-check map that identifies both coverage and evidence gaps.

Findings that can be reproduced

Can another reviewer follow the setup, input and observed behaviour for a reported problem and understand why it matters to intended use?

You would see: Ask for reproducible findings with inputs, expected behaviour and actual results.

A decision with accountable owners

Are unresolved failures visible to the people who decide on release, with reasons to proceed, limit use or hold the change?

You would see: Read the release review record and the owners’ decisions on outstanding findings.

Checks beyond the release date

Are monitoring, escalation and incident responsibilities defined for the delivered scope so new evidence can inform subsequent decisions?

You would see: Review the monitoring plan, escalation steps and named operating owners.

How we'll achieve it

  1. Agree

    Identify requirements and risks

    Clarify intended users, uses and failure consequences with the decision owners. Include relevant policies and requirements supplied by their advisers.

    Output: Requirements and risk register with agreed evaluation scope.

  2. Inspect

    Examine the existing evidence

    Review current tests, evaluations and known failures. Identify which claims have evidence and where the intended use demands additional checks.

    Output: Evidence inventory and a map of untested requirements.

  3. Evaluate

    Run the scoped checks

    Test relevant functionality, reliability and access controls. For AI, evaluate appropriate output quality and failure cases using agreed examples.

    Output: Test and evaluation results with inputs, expected behaviour and observations.

  4. Report

    Make findings and gaps explicit

    Explain reproducible problems, the risks they affect and the limits of the assessment. Keep unresolved findings visible alongside passing checks.

    Output: Findings report with reproduction steps, evidence gaps and follow-up priorities.

  5. Prepare

    Support the release and operating decision

    Review the evidence with release owners and prepare monitoring and escalation plans. Record conditions for release, restricted use or further investigation.

    Output: Release decision checklist, monitoring plan and incident escalation responsibilities.

Practice we build on

Collect evidence at different layers — The Practical Test Pyramid

The Practical Test Pyramid describes tests at different levels of detail, with many focused checks and fewer broad ones. A mix helps examine individual rules, integrations and user-facing behaviour.

Use a reliability target to guide decisions — Google SRE Workbook: Implementing SLOs

Service level objectives set reliability targets; an error budget is the permitted unreliability over the measurement window. The SRE Workbook uses these to inform decisions about reliability work and further change.

Look at failure and recovery together — DORA’s software delivery metrics

In DORA’s original four keys, change failure rate concerns the share of changes that cause production failures, while time to restore service concerns recovery after failure. Together they inform a review of release stability.

Evaluate AI risks in context — NIST AI Risk Management Framework

Measure is a function of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework concerned with assessing, analyzing and tracking AI risks. It informs evaluation for intended use alongside the framework’s governance and risk-management work.

In scope for this service

Who this fits

Teams assessing a prototype, planned release or existing system against requirements for real use.

What the work can cover

Identify risks and test against intended use; define review/release criteria and responsibilities; prepare monitoring and incident-response plans appropriate to the agreed scope.

Typical deliverables

A menu to agree in scope, rather than a fixed package.

  • A risk assessment tied to the system and its intended use
  • Tests for functionality, reliability, access controls, and relevant AI failure cases
  • Review and release checklists with named decision owners
  • A monitoring and incident-response plan with escalation steps

How an engagement can work

  1. Agree requirements and risks
  2. inspect existing evidence
  3. run scoped tests/evaluations
  4. report findings and evidence gaps
  5. prepare a release/operation decision with owners

What we need to understand

  • The system design, intended users, and planned uses
  • Existing policies and relevant requirements from your advisers
  • Known failure cases and the people responsible for review

Start with a description. Agree data handling and access before sharing confidential materials.

Expected outcome and boundaries

An evidence-based decision, including reasons to hold a release. Evaluate coverage of agreed risks, reproducible findings and clarity of ownership; passing checks establishes only what was tested.

Choose measures and acceptance criteria together before building. These are aims and evaluation questions, not guaranteed results.

Scope and constraints

Assessment does not certify legal compliance, eliminate all failures or guarantee safe operation. A monitoring plan does not mean Kheya operates monitoring or provides an incident-response service.

Deliverables, access, responsibilities, schedule, pricing, IP terms, and any further support belong in the agreed scope.

Questions

Must we have an AI system?

This applies to software as well as AI.

Can evaluation find that we should not release?

Yes; unresolved failures and missing evidence belong in the decision.

Is this service only for AI systems?

Software applications also need evidence for functionality, reliability and access controls. AI-specific evaluation belongs in scope when the system includes behaviour that requires it.

Can the review conclude that we should hold a release?

A hold is a valid outcome when failures or missing evidence affect intended use. The report should explain what remains unresolved and who decides whether further work is sufficient.

Does a monitoring plan include ongoing operation?

A plan describes measures, responsibilities and escalation steps. Operating monitoring or providing incident response would require a separate agreement with explicit responsibilities.
