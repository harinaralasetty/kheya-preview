Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE)

Build alongside the team doing the work

Work with users and system owners to integrate a scoped solution into its actual workflow. Agree collaboration, access and acceptance responsibilities before iterating on working changes.

Engineers working alongside a client’s team to integrate and deliver solutions within its real workflows.

A solution needs to fit the way our team actually works. Requirements, integrations and acceptance become clearer through work with the team in its real workflow.

The idea behind this service

The embedded loop

Observe real work, build an agreed slice, deploy within scope and learn with the people using it.

The embedded loop keeps engineering close to the workflow it serves. The team and engineer observe a task, build a scoped change, put it into the agreed environment and review what happens, using that evidence to refine the next increment or prepare handover.

What it is

Forward Deployed Engineering emphasizes working alongside a team to understand and deliver within its real workflow. The work may involve features, integrations or automation; the distinguishing part is the close connection between implementation and feedback from the people using it.

The collaboration arrangement is scoped rather than an open-ended staffing offer. Alongside does not promise dedicated staffing, onsite availability or unrestricted system access; staffing, access, support, service levels, IP terms, timeline and pricing must be agreed.

What winning looks like

A shared view of the real task

Can participating users and system owners describe the same workflow, its exceptions and the part of it that the engineer is authorized to change?

You would see: Review the workflow map and collaboration plan with the participating owners.

A slice that fits its surroundings

Does the implemented change interact with the surrounding systems as expected when used for an agreed task in the approved environment?

You would see: Observe an agreed workflow check and inspect the connected systems’ behaviour.

Feedback that changes the work

Are review observations translated into specific refinements or explicit decisions to defer, instead of accumulating as an unexamined request list?

You would see: Compare the review log with implemented refinements and recorded scope decisions.

A receiving team ready for its scope

Can the receiving team explain how to operate the delivered work, handle known exceptions and seek any support that has actually been agreed?

You would see: Walk through the handover materials, exception instructions and ownership record.

How we'll achieve it

  1. Boundaries

    Agree the workflow and collaboration

    Choose the workflow, participating team and acceptance owners. Agree collaboration arrangements, approved access and data handling before work begins.

    Output: Scoped work plan with collaboration boundaries, access rules and responsibilities.

  2. Observe

    Work with users and system owners

    Follow representative tasks and exceptions with the people doing them. Inspect the surrounding systems and identify the constraints on an initial change.

    Output: Workflow and integration map with observed constraints and task examples.

  3. Integrate

    Build and deploy the agreed slice

    Implement the selected feature or integration within approved systems. Prepare deployment into the agreed environment with the responsible system owners.

    Output: Working slice with deployment notes and records of approved integration access.

  4. Learn

    Review the workflow and refine

    Review the change during agreed real tasks, then refine it against the evidence. Treat additions beyond scope as decisions for the responsible owners.

    Output: Workflow check results, review observations and a record of scoped refinements.

  5. Handover

    Transfer the work and close the loop

    Document the delivered scope and remaining exceptions with the receiving team. Agree operating responsibility and discuss any further engagement separately.

    Output: Handover materials, operating ownership record and list of remaining work.

Practice we build on

Engineering close to the workflow — Forward Deployed Engineer

The Forward Deployed Engineer role is documented as engineering work close to the organization using the software, involving implementation and integration around its needs. The term alone does not define an engagement’s staffing or commercial terms.

Review working increments — Continuous Delivery

Continuous delivery supports small changes kept in a deployable state. This gives a collaborating team working software to review as it decides what to develop next.

Use feedback to choose the next change — DORA’s software delivery metrics

DORA describes small batches and a shared review of delivery constraints, followed by an improvement and a check of progress. That cycle offers a way to organize feedback around the delivery process.

Teams whose delivery problem needs close collaboration with the people doing the work and the owners of the surrounding systems.

What the work can cover

What we need to understand

  • A workflow owner and participating team
  • representative tasks and exceptions

  • decision, acceptance and operating responsibilities

Start with a description. Agree data handling and access before sharing confidential materials.

Expected outcome and boundaries

A solution evaluated in the context where it will be used. Select evidence such as completion of an agreed real task, integration behavior, exception handling and the receiving team's ability to operate the delivered scope.

Choose measures and acceptance criteria together before building. These are aims and evaluation questions, not guaranteed results.

Scope and constraints

“Alongside” does not establish dedicated staffing or onsite availability. It does not authorize unrestricted system access. Staffing, collaboration arrangements, access, operating responsibility, support, service levels, IP terms, timeline and pricing must be agreed; none is promised here.

Deliverables, access, responsibilities, schedule, pricing, IP terms, and any further support belong in the agreed scope.

Questions

Is FDE another name for custom development?

It can involve development, but its distinguishing emphasis is alongside-the-team integration and delivery within real workflows.

Is it only automation?

It can involve integrations, features or other solutions; the workflow determines the work.

What happens after handover?

Operational ownership and any further engagement need explicit agreement.

Does alongside mean a dedicated engineer on site?

Dedicated staffing and onsite availability are not established by this service description. Staffing, collaboration arrangements, timeline and pricing need agreement for the specific engagement.

Does this arrangement grant access to all our systems?

Only explicitly approved access is in scope, with agreed data-handling rules and system owners. Close collaboration does not authorize unrestricted access or actions beyond those boundaries.

How is this different from a software build?

Custom development defines the application being delivered. This approach emphasizes observing, integrating and reviewing alongside the people working in the actual workflow; it can include development when that task requires it.

What happens when the scoped work is handed over?

The receiving owner takes the agreed operating responsibilities. Support, service levels, IP terms and any further work need explicit agreement rather than being implied by the collaboration.
