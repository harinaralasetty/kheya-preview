The idea behind this service

The decision on record

Make the choice, its trade-offs and its reasons available to everyone responsible for it.

A technology choice becomes a decision on record when owners can see the options, criteria and trade-offs together. Record the assumptions and what would change your mind, so a recommendation can be challenged before spending and revisited when circumstances change.

What it is

Technology advisory turns an unresolved choice into a written recommendation that owners can examine. Start with the work people need to do, the constraints on that work and the cost of leaving it unchanged.

The scope is a decision and a next-step brief, which may call for a purchase, a build, a process change or further investigation. Advice is not implementation or proof of feasibility; estimates depend on the information available and uncertain assumptions remain visible.

What winning looks like

A shared decision

Can the people funding, using and operating the proposed change describe the same problem and the choice in front of them?

You would see: Ask for a decision statement with named owners and their review notes.

Comparable alternatives

Do the realistic options use the same criteria, with cost assumptions and dependencies exposed rather than hidden in a preferred answer?

You would see: Inspect an option comparison that includes reasons to pursue or defer each choice.

Visible uncertainty

Which assumptions could overturn the recommendation, and what evidence would help the owners resolve them before committing?

You would see: Review an assumption log with open questions, evidence needed and responsible people.

An actionable next step

Does the recommendation end in a bounded task with ownership and acceptance criteria, including a reason to pause if evidence is missing?

You would see: Read the next-step brief and check that an owner can accept or decline its scope.

How we'll achieve it

  1. Frame

    Name the decision

    Agree the business goal, who decides and the constraints that make the choice difficult. Separate the question from any preferred technology.

    Output: Decision statement with owners, constraints and review criteria.

  2. Inspect

    Walk through the current work

    Review the workflow, available tools and known limitations with the people involved. Record information gaps that affect cost or feasibility.

    Output: Workflow notes and an inventory of assumptions and missing information.

  3. Compare

    Set out realistic options

    Compare a build, purchase, change to an existing system or process-only response where relevant. Use the agreed criteria for each option.

    Output: Ranked option comparison with cost assumptions, dependencies and risks.

  4. Review

    Challenge the trade-offs

    Review the consequences with decision owners, including what each option leaves unresolved and what new evidence could change the recommendation.

    Output: Reviewed decision record with trade-offs and conditions for reconsideration.

  5. Agree

    Turn the recommendation into a brief

    Agree a recommendation or a focused investigation when evidence is insufficient. Define the next task and who will judge whether it is complete.

    Output: Next-step project brief with scope, ownership and acceptance criteria.

Practice we build on

A reason behind each choice — Architectural Decision Records

An architectural decision record is a short account of a significant design choice and its rationale, trade-offs and consequences. A decision log keeps those reasons available for later review.

Keep the decision history — Documenting Architecture Decisions

Nygard describes records with context, a decision, status and consequences. Keeping superseded records helps future readers understand why an earlier choice was made.

Test an assumption in a small step — Continuous Delivery

Continuous delivery keeps software ready for release and supports small batches of working changes. Feedback from a limited change can inform the next commitment.

Separate migration choices — AWS migration strategies

AWS distinguishes strategies such as retaining, retiring, repurchasing and refactoring a system. This vocabulary helps a team discuss different migration choices rather than assume everything needs rebuilding.

In scope for this service

Who this fits

People responsible for a technology decision, with a business goal but uncertain priorities or implementation options.

What the work can cover

Understand the decision and workflow; compare realistic options, constraints and risks; define a practical next step. Include AI only when relevant to the problem.

Typical deliverables

A menu to agree in scope, rather than a fixed package.

  • A ranked shortlist of use cases, with reasons to pursue or defer each one
  • A build-versus-buy comparison with cost assumptions and dependencies
  • A feasibility and risk assessment for the selected workflow
  • A project brief with scope, ownership, and measurable acceptance criteria

How an engagement can work

  1. Frame the decision
  2. inspect the current work
  3. compare options
  4. review tradeoffs with owners
  5. agree a recommendation and next-step brief

What we need to understand

  • The business problem and how you measure it today
  • A walkthrough of the workflow and the tools involved
  • Budget, data-access constraints, and the people responsible for decisions

Start with a description. Agree data handling and access before sharing confidential materials.

Expected outcome and boundaries

A decision that can be explained and tested. Evaluate whether key alternatives, assumptions, dependencies and unresolved questions are explicit.

Choose measures and acceptance criteria together before building. These are aims and evaluation questions, not guaranteed results.

Scope and constraints

An advisory recommendation is not implementation or proof that an option will succeed. Costs and feasibility depend on supplied information; unresolved assumptions stay visible.

Deliverables, access, responsibilities, schedule, pricing, IP terms, and any further support belong in the agreed scope.

Questions

Do we need a chosen technology?

No; the decision can be the starting point.

Will this lead to a build?

The brief may recommend a build, purchase, process change or further investigation.

Must we already have a preferred technology?

Bring the decision and its constraints, even if the options are unclear. A technology shortlist can follow a review of the workflow and the available evidence.

Does advisory commit us to a build?

The recommendation may support buying a tool, changing a process, keeping the current arrangement or investigating further. Implementation would need its own agreed scope.

How do we revisit the recommendation?

Keep the original reasons and assumptions with the decision. A change in budget, requirements or feasibility can trigger a new review without erasing the earlier context.
